# DS2500-Fast-Fashion-Impact

# Group Members: Amina Aitnouri, Anya Krishnamony, Siya Patel

# This project investigates the environmental impact of fast fashion brands using a combination of data science methods and sustainability metrics. Drawing on a dataset of over 1,000 global fashion brands from Gigasheet, we analyzed key metrics such as carbon footprint, water usage, waste production, and material types. Our goal was to evaluate whether sustainability ratings and pricing align with actual environmental performance.

# Using techniques like time series aggregation, categorical comparisons and a KNN classifier, we uncovered several findings. Sustainability ratings did not consistently correspond with lower emissions, and there was no correlation between product price and carbon footprint. Our KNN model, trained on aggregated country-level features by average, successfully classified countries like Norway into emission categories, revealing regional sustainability trends. We also explored the role of material type in water usage, challenging common assumptions about ‘eco-friendly’ fabrics.

# Ultimately, our analysis suggests that self-reported sustainability efforts may not reflect real environmental outcomes. Future work could explore the impact of shifting trade policies, such as U.S. tariff reinstatements, on supply chain emissions and sustainability investment, emphasizing the need for transparency for such an important and discussed cause.
